Research

Three programs, one measurement problem.

How do you ask a biological sample many chemical questions at the same time, without destroying it? Every program in the lab is a version of that question, applied to a different tissue and a different clinical decision.

What connects them is the probe: a plasmonic core, a Raman reporter chosen by calculation, a protective silica shell, and a targeting ligand. Change the shell chemistry, the size, or the readout, and the same molecular idea serves a brain section, a microliter of spinal fluid, or an operating room.

BRAINS · Brain Raman Analysis of Immune and Neuropathology Signatures

Multiplexed Raman profiling of brain–immune interactions

The problem

Immune dysregulation is a driver of neurodegeneration, not a bystander. Microglia and other immune populations interact directly with amyloid plaques, tau tangles, and α-synuclein aggregates, and similar remodelling appears across Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, and normal aging.

Yet the tools for high-dimensional immune profiling in intact brain tissue remain limited. Conventional immunohistochemistry gives one or a few markers per section; each additional question costs another slide, and the spatial relationships between them are lost.

What we are building

A nanoparticle-based SERS imaging platform capable of simultaneously detecting up to 65 biomarkers on a single section, at subcellular resolution, leaving the tissue available for proteomics and genomics afterwards.

  • Aim 1 Design spectrally unique probes Combine computationally guided reporter selection with gold nanostar cores to push multiplexing capacity beyond 65 probes.
  • Aim 2 Make targeting specific and reproducible Optimised antibody–nanoparticle conjugation plus microfluidic-assisted staining of FFPE brain sections, for binding precision and throughput.
  • Aim 3 Map real tissue Highly multiplexed Raman imaging of postmortem Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and aging brain tissue, relating immune phenotypes to neuropathological hallmarks in space.

Impact. BRAINS aims to produce the first comprehensive, non-destructive molecular maps of brain–immune interaction — useful both for mechanistic discovery and as a dense dataset for machine-learning biomarker discovery and computational modelling of neuroinflammation.

02

CNS liquid biopsy

Early, minimally invasive immune profiling of neuroinflammation

A dual-mode platform reading cerebrospinal fluid two ways at once.

The problem

Neuroinflammation is a unifying driver of neurodegeneration and age-related decline. From Alzheimer's and Parkinson's to multiple sclerosis and traumatic brain injury, maladaptive immune responses accelerate neuronal dysfunction, while subtle shifts in immune activation and extracellular vesicle composition contribute to “inflammaging.”

Current cerebrospinal fluid assays — ELISA, flow cytometry, proteomics — need large sample volumes and cannot resolve the cellular and vesicle heterogeneity that reflects early immune dysregulation. The signal that matters arrives before the symptoms, in a sample too small to spend.

What we are building

The first dual-mode CNS liquid biopsy platform, integrating label-free Raman fingerprinting with multiplexed nanotag phenotyping — capturing both the intrinsic biochemical state and the surface marker profile of CSF-derived immune cells and extracellular vesicles.

  • Aim 1 Design the sensing materials Ag/Au SERS nanomaterials serving both label-free classification of cells and vesicles and multiplexed nanotags against microglia-like cells, monocytes, T cells, and CNS-derived vesicles.
  • Aim 2 Classify with dual readout Machine-learning classification and spectral molecular profiling based on combined Raman and fluorescence detection.
  • Aim 3 Compare across disease Immune signatures in CSF from Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, multiple sclerosis, and mild cognitive impairment cohorts, separating shared from disease-specific pathways.

Impact. Comprehensive molecular readouts from microliter-scale CSF would allow neuroinflammatory signatures to be detected before symptomatic onset, patients to be stratified for emerging immunotherapies, and treatment response to be monitored longitudinally with a minimally invasive sample.

03

Sentinel lymph node mapping

Multiplexed nanoparticle immune profiling in the operating room

Renal-clearable probes that guide the surgeon, then report on what was found.

The problem

Immune balance in the sentinel lymph node dictates progression and therapy response. Accurate identification of these nodes is critical for staging in melanoma, head and neck, and breast cancer — yet current mapping relies on non-specific dyes or radiotracers that locate the node without saying anything about its immune state.

In the immunotherapy era that is a serious limitation. The surgeon learns where but not what, and the immunological question is answered days later, if at all.

What we are building

Renal-clearable multimodal nanoprobes that combine PET and NIR-II fluorescence for intra-operative localisation with Raman barcodes for high-plex ex vivo immune phenotyping — one particle, two jobs, in sequence.

  • Aim 1 Engineer the particle Renal-clearable, PEGylated silica-encapsulated probes under 10 nm carrying NIR-II fluorophores and PET tracers, for efficient lymphatic uptake and clearance.
  • Aim 2 Build the immune panel Multiplexed Raman panel conjugating SERS nanoparticles to antibodies against CD8, FoxP3, PD-L1, and related markers.
  • Aim 3 Close the loop in vivo Integrate PET/NIR-II localisation with ex vivo SERS immune profiling in orthotopic tumour models, correlating immune composition with therapeutic response.

Impact. Mapping sentinel nodes while simultaneously determining their immunological status would reduce unnecessary lymphadenectomies, enable precision-guided immunotherapy stratification, and generalise to immune monitoring in transplantation, infection, and autoimmune disease.

Where the program is heading

The three programs are designed to feed each other, and to generate the kind of data that makes computational discovery possible.

Direction 01

Past 65 channels

Expanding sensitive multiplexing beyond 65 channels by co-designing Raman reporters, nanoparticle architectures, and acquisition schemes together, using computational and high-throughput optimisation rather than one at a time.

Direction 02

AI-native analysis

Pipelines for spectral unmixing, confidence scoring, and uncertainty quantification — turning dense molecular data into biomarkers a clinician could act on, with an honest error bar attached.

Direction 03

Deployable, not just publishable

Reducing sample volume, runtime, and operator burden while holding performance across instruments, so that a platform developed on one microscope survives contact with a second one.

Work on this

Each program has room for a student or postdoc who wants to own a piece of it end to end — the synthesis, the imaging, and the analysis.
