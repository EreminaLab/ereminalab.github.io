Biography

Her doctoral work at Lomonosov Moscow State University addressed a stubborn problem in surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy: many of the most clinically important analytes are effectively invisible to it. Catecholamines absorb in the ultraviolet, far above the plasmon band of silver or gold, so they produce no useful signal.

The MIRRACLE approach, developed there, solves that chemically rather than optically — Cu(II)-mediated ternary complexes and charge-transfer chemistry shift the absorption into resonance, bringing dopamine, norepinephrine, and epinephrine within reach at femtomolar concentrations. The same idea extended to environmental carcinogens in matrices as difficult as gasoline and freshwater.

As an Agilent Postdoctoral Fellow and then NIBIB K99/R00 fellow at the University of Southern California, with Cristina Zavaleta and Peter Yingxiao Wang, she turned to the multiplexing problem: how many distinguishable labels can one measurement carry? She established a computational–experimental framework for reporter selection, built a 26-colour SERS nanoparticle library, and performed the first noninvasive 26-plex SERS imaging in a living mouse.

As a visiting scholar at Stanford with Parag Mallick she developed quantitative workflows for hyper-multiplexed proteomics, and with Scott Fraser's group at USC worked on phasor-space approaches to spectral unmixing — the computational half of the platform.

She has published 37 peer-reviewed articles and holds six patents. Her review of nanoparticle-enabled immune profiling was named Nanoscale Horizons' Outstanding Review of 2024.
